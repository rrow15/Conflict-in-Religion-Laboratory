The Conflict in Religion Research Group (CORE) is a research laboratory dedicated to understanding religion through the lens of psychology, particularly how religious and nonreligious worldviews shape human thought, emotion, behavior, and social relationships. As psychology of religion researchers, we examine religion not only as a belief system or an institution, but as a psychological phenomenon embedded in culture and individual experience. We look into both religiosity, the performance of religion, and religiousness, the psychological process that involves believing, bonding, behaving, and belonging. Situated in the Philippines and informed by cross-cultural perspectives, CORE considers how these phenomena operate within different social, cultural, and historical contexts.

We explore conflict—differences in orientation and even opposition—within and among religious groups in the following arenas: shared spaces, identity and agency, as well as mental health and coping. Our work ranges from anti-atheist prejudice, religious pluralism, polyculturalism, and conversion from high-cost religious groups to the impact of religion and religious identity on clinical outcomes and therapeutic alliance, with more projects underway. 

Through these endeavors, CORE aims to approach the complex world of religion and spirituality with scientific rigor, cultural humility, and competence, applying social, emotional, and cognitive psychological methods to generate more knowledge of these constructs and create opportunities for further research on these phenomena.
